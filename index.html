Default vs strongest models: what changed?

Does paying for an AI’s strongest model change the advice a leader gets? I asked three AIs the same two leadership questions, first on their everyday (default) model, then on their strongest. For Claude, the strongest model changed the advice. For ChatGPT, it barely did.

The two questions: what should a Singapore bank chief executive prioritise in the next 90 days on AI agents (AI that carries out tasks, not just answers), and how much effort should go into bottom-up AI, where teams try things for themselves? All answers were generated on 11 Oct 2026. The page follows the order the tests were run: Stage 1 the defaults, Stage 2 the strongest, Stage 3 all five side by side.

The models

  • Claude: Claude Sonnet 5.5 = default (“Sonnet”); Claude Opus 5.5 = strongest (“Opus”).
  • ChatGPT: ChatGPT GPT-6.1-Sol = default (“Sol”); ChatGPT GPT-6-Astra at high reasoning = strongest (“Astra”). High reasoning means it is set to think longer before answering.
  • Grok Bot: one answer, labelled Grok Bot (11 Oct 2026). The same text appears in every stage.

Grok Bot doesn't use one fixed model. It picks a model for each task and doesn't show which one it used (Grok Bot's help page). So its answer is labelled by date, not by model version.

How to read this. The left column always says Agree or Split first, so you don’t need colour. Green rows are one wide cell: every AI in that stage said the same. Orange rows give each AI its own cell. In Stage 3, each strongest-model cell carries a tag: Same means it matches its own default model, Changed means the stronger model gave different advice.

In short: Opus moved away from Sonnet, from “list every agent first” to “pilot one or two back-office (internal, not customer-facing) jobs and prove it”. Astra gave almost word for word what Sol gave.

Same leader and guardrails, three different first moves

All three put one senior leader in charge, with clear limits and a way to stop the agents. Then they part: Sonnet lists every agent in use, Sol runs a small trial, and Grok Bot runs one painful process for 90 days beside the people who do it today.

Grok Bot is the only default that builds in people

Staff own the results. Customer satisfaction is part of the weekly test. Whoever finds the win gets recognition. Its main risk is leaders who don’t listen, so a good idea dies in the team that found it.

Upgrading Claude changed the advice; upgrading ChatGPT did not

Opus replaced Sonnet’s “list every agent” with “prove one or two jobs”. Astra matched Sol on both questions. When the model choice can change the recommendation, ask both versions before you treat the answer as settled.

Opus and Astra now give similar headline numbers

Both keep a human approving anything touching money or customers, and both put about 30% into bottom-up work. They still differ in stance: Opus says prove it, Astra says start small.

The differences left are in emphasis and detail

On these two broad questions, the strongest answers mostly differ in what they count and what they fear. To see a sharper contrast, ask about something only your organisation knows, such as your own complaints backlog or a decision you face this quarter.

Grok Bot still stands apart on people

No Claude or ChatGPT answer had staff own the results, put customer satisfaction in the weekly test, reward whoever finds the win, or name leaders who don’t listen as the main risk.

All answers were generated on 11 Oct 2026. Each AI answered each question once, and the same AI can answer differently next time. Ask again two or three times before you teach from these answers.
